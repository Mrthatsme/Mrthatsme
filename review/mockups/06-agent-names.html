What you're looking at: proposed names for your five automations and the Daily Brief, to replace the generic names AI tools hand everyone. Each one is a real word, each letter says what it does, and read in order they tell the story of one job. Nothing is renamed until you pick.

Option 1 · recommended

Six names, one job, start to finish.

FIRST answers. TRACE follows up. CLOCK keeps the day. STARS asks for the review. PAID gets the invoice out. WATCH tells you all of it every morning.

One line that works on the home page, a business card, or a sales call.

Replaces "Lead Catcher"

FIRST
  • Fields every new inquiry
  • In your words, in minutes
  • Replies to chat, forms and email
  • Saves the lead with name and number
  • Tees up your callback

The first business to reply usually gets the job. That's the whole idea.

Replaces "Quote Chaser"

TRACE
  • Tracks every open quote
  • Reminds on day 2, 5 and 10
  • Asks politely, never pushes
  • Catches the reply
  • Ends the moment they answer

No quote goes missing. You can trace every one.

Replaces "Schedule Keeper"

CLOCK
  • Confirms the day before
  • Lines up tomorrow's schedule
  • Organizes reschedules
  • Catches no-shows the same day
  • Keeps your crews on time

Your day runs on time, and it fits the name Minutes Regained.

Replaces "Review Builder"

STARS
  • Sends
  • The
  • Ask
  • Right after
  • Service

One polite ask the day the job closes. Never a second.

Replaces "Invoice & Chase"

PAID
  • Prompt invoices when the job closes
  • Automatic, polite reminders
  • Instant view of who's paid
  • Dollars go straight to you, never through us

Says exactly what the owner wants, and the D says what you never do.

Replaces "The Daily Brief"

WATCH
  • What got handled
  • Awaiting your OK
  • Trouble we caught and fixed
  • Calls to return
  • Highlights of the day

Your morning summary. It ties to your tagline: Always on watch.

Option 2

One word for the whole system: WATCH.

Instead of six names, the five automations become the five letters of one word, and the brief becomes the Watch Report.

W

Welcome

Every new lead answered in minutes.

A

Appointments

Confirmed, reminded, no-shows followed up.

T

Track quotes

Day 2, 5 and 10 follow-ups, stopped on reply.

C

Chase invoices

Sent on job close, unpaid ones followed up.

H

Happy customers

One review ask the day the job closes.

The Watch Report

Every morning: what WATCH handled and what needs you.

Option 1 is stronger for selling. Each automation has its own name you can point to on a call ("STARS asks for the review"), and the six-name line tells the whole story in one breath.
Option 2 is simpler. One word to remember, tied straight to "Always on watch." The trade-off is that individual services lose their own names.
Before you lock any name in: run a quick trademark search at USPTO.gov (free) for each name in the "business automation / software services" category. Common words used as product names are usually fine, but check. I avoided SWIFT on purpose, because it's a well-known banking network.
